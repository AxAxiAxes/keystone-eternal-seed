# KEYSTONE architectural equilibrium symbolism

**Status:** Founder-stated design symbolism, recorded as concept/philosophy,
not verified structural-engineering or historical fact
**Recorded:** 2026-09-18

## Founder's statement

> "The first and last stone in architecture symbolizes a point of equality
> and balance, where salt meets water, where the weight of the foundation
> equals the weight of its structure, equilibrium from negative to
> positive... the stone that bears all."

This is recorded verbatim as the founder's stated symbolic reading of the
"first and last stone" of a structure (a cornerstone-and-keystone pairing),
shared alongside the "Honor of Eternity" crest image (see
`docs/keystone/assets/honor-of-eternity-crest.jpg`,
`KEYSTONE-CREATIVE-ORIGIN-000002`).

## What this is, and is not

- **Real, verifiable fact:** in conventional masonry construction, a
  **cornerstone** is the first stone set, from which every other
  measurement is aligned, and a **keystone** is the final wedge-shaped
  stone placed at the crown of an arch that locks the surrounding stones
  under compression, so the arch bears load without mortar. Both are real
  structural terms; a keystone genuinely does "bear" the arch's load in the
  sense that removing it causes the arch to collapse.
- **Founder's symbolic extension (recorded, not verified):** the reading
  that the first and last stone represent an "equality and balance" point,
  "where salt meets water," and "equilibrium from negative to positive" is
  the founder's own metaphorical framework, not an established
  architectural, physical, or historical claim. It is recorded here as the
  KEYSTONE brand's internal symbolic vocabulary — consistent with how this
  repository already treats the KEYSTONE name itself as symbolic naming for
  the project's own persistence/continuity architecture (see
  `docs/keystone/CERTIFICATE_ETERNAL_ORIGIN_ANCHOR_AND_EVALUATION.md`), not
  as a load-bearing engineering claim about any real building.
