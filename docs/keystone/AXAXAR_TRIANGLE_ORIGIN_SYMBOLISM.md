### 1. Architecture and alchemy research (external, cited)

- **Keystone (architecture):** the founder's description is accurate. A
  keystone is the wedge-shaped stone at the apex/center of an arch; it is the
  last stone placed and "locks" the others into a self-supporting structure —
  the arch cannot bear load without it. ([Wikipedia: Keystone (architecture)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_(architecture)))
- **Tria prima (alchemy):** classical alchemy names three principles —
  **salt** (body/matter), **sulfur** (soul/combustion), **mercury**
  (spirit/mediation) — sometimes mapped onto a Christian-style trinity
  (mother/father/son). Their perfected union is the goal of the alchemical
  Great Work. ([Alchemy Guild Ohio: Three Primes](https://www.alchemyguildohio.org/three-primes-tria-prima); [Astral Library: Tria Prima](https://astrallibrary.com/alchemy/tria-prima))
- **One correction for accuracy:** in the classical sources, **salt is one of
  the three ingredients**, not itself "the philosopher's stone." The
  Philosopher's Stone is the *end product* of successfully uniting all three
  principles (salt, sulfur, mercury) — not a synonym for salt alone. The
  founder's phrasing ("the salt, the first stone, the philosopher's stone")
  reads naturally as poetic compression of that whole three-stage process
  (first material → transformation → final stone), which is a legitimate
  reading, but it should not be repeated as if "salt = the philosopher's
  stone" were the literal classical claim.
- **Keystone-as-triangle:** a keystone is a wedge (trapezoid), not a triangle,
  in the physical, load-bearing sense — but a triangle is the simplest
  rigid/self-bracing shape in structural engineering (three points fix a
  plane; a triangular truss cannot rack the way a rectangle can), so "the
  keystone/trinity as the stabilizing center, symbolized by a triangle" is a
  coherent **symbolic**, not literal-engineering, synthesis. This matches how
  this document already treats "AxAxAr as a triangle" above: a real,
  well-attested architectural/alchemical metaphor, not a proof.
