# AXES Creator Origin Constitution

**Status:** Internal operating constitution  
**Recorded:** 2026-09-10  
**Owner:** Founder or a named human delegate  
**Applies to:** AXES, Chichetki, XIIOM, AXOUS (through the AXOUX site), all
source archives, creator registries, public assets, research records, and
AI-assisted work.

## Article I — Purpose and limits

This constitution protects accurate creator credit, consent, evidence, and
correction across AXES work. It establishes an internal recordkeeping and
decision framework. It does not itself determine legal authorship, copyright
ownership, trademark rights, patentability, priority, employment status, or
contract rights. Those questions require the applicable records, agreements,
and qualified advice in the relevant jurisdiction.

No system may describe an origin record, registry entry, hash, timestamp,
signature, AI output, external database result, or repeated online claim as
conclusive proof of real-world ownership or truth.

## Article II — Governing principles

1. **Creator dignity:** Credit and consent belong with the person or entity
   that created, contributed, or lawfully controls the material; AXES does not
   appropriate contributor work through access, submission, processing, or
   publication.
2. **Evidence before assertion:** Material claims need a traceable source and
   an explicit confidence level. Unverified material is labeled as unverified,
   not silently promoted to fact.
3. **Human authority:** A named human approves public attribution,
   publication, correction decisions, and dispute outcomes. Automation can
   organize evidence but cannot adjudicate rights or publish contested work.
4. **Minimum disclosure:** Preserve evidence privately whenever public
   disclosure would reveal personal data, unpublished work, account
   information, security details, or other protected material.
5. **Correction and challenge:** Origin records remain versioned, reviewable,
   and correctable. Immutability is not a reason to preserve an error.
6. **No retaliation:** A good-faith contributor, subject, or rights holder may
   request correction, removal, or review without losing access to unrelated
   services or being publicly discredited.

## Article III — Evidence and confidence standard

Every material origin claim must be classified before it is used internally or
publicly.

| Class | Meaning | Permitted statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder-provided | Founder supplied the direction or material and the private source record identifies it | "Founder-provided project source material" |
| Contributor-confirmed | A contributor or authorized representative confirmed their contribution and intended use | "Credited with permission for the stated use" |
| Licensed or permission-based | A record identifies the applicable license or permission and its limits | "Used under documented license or permission" |
| Collaborative synthesis | Multiple people or approved tools informed an interpretation or structure | "Collaborative synthesis" |
| Technical assessment | A conclusion from reproducible technical evidence | "Technical assessment based on recorded evidence" |
| External or unverified | A third-party statement, historical record, or research lead that lacks sufficient review | "Unverified external information" |
| Contested | A claim subject to an unresolved, credible challenge | "Attribution under review; not for public use" |

The attribution register must record evidence quality separately from source
identity. A valid file hash or signed provenance record can demonstrate
tamper-evidence for the captured record; it does not independently establish
that the underlying assertion is true, complete, or legally controlling.

## Article IV — Required origin record

Every material asset, insight, research conclusion, design, media work, code
contribution, or public claim requires an origin record before public release.
The record must contain:

1. A stable internal identifier and concise description.
2. Origin class and the exact claim being recorded.
3. Creator, contributor, or source as known, including whether identity is
   confirmed, self-asserted, unknown, or intentionally withheld.
4. Source location, capture date, record date, and a content hash where
   appropriate.
5. Rights status, intended use, consent or license reference, and any
   expiration or use limitation.
6. Evidence links or private archive references, with access classification.
7. A confidence level, known uncertainty, and the human reviewer.
8. Public credit wording, if any, and accessibility treatment for published
   media.
9. Version history, correction/removal requests, and the disposition of each.

Private source records and identifying evidence remain outside the public
repository unless publication is specifically approved and lawful.

## Article V — Creator consent and authority

- Obtain documented permission for the exact public context before publishing
  a contributor's work, identity, biography, or attribution.
- Record whether a creator requests a name, pseudonym, collective credit, no
  public credit, or a later review before publication.
- Do not infer a license, transfer of ownership, endorsement, employment
  relationship, or permission for AI training from a conversation, preview,
  upload, interview, or directory listing.
- A creator may request factual corrections or removal review. AXES will
  acknowledge receipt, preserve the request privately, pause disputed public
  use when warranted, and respond through a named human owner.
- Only an authorized rights holder, contributor, or designated human owner
  may approve an exception to a documented usage restriction.

## Article VI — Research and external intelligence integrity

AXES research may support decisions only when each source is labeled by its
origin and confidence. External intelligence is a lead until reviewed; it is
not project memory, creator evidence, or authority merely because it is
searchable, popular, machine-generated, signed, or attributed to a known
organization.

For every material research conclusion, retain the question, source URL or
archive reference, retrieval date, quoted or summarized evidence, reviewer,
conflicts, and conclusion confidence. Compare independent sources for
high-impact claims. Cite primary records when available. Preserve contrary
evidence rather than suppressing it.

AI-generated or AI-summarized research must identify the provider/tool,
prompt or task scope where practical, source set, human reviewer, and
limitations. It may not invent citations, origin, consent, ownership,
performance, competitor activity, market facts, or creator intent. A human
must verify cited material before relying on it for publication, contract,
rights, safety, financial, professional, or reputational decisions.

## Article VII — Asset provenance and technical signals

Use hashes, version history, access logs, signed manifests, or compatible
content-provenance standards when proportionate to the asset and risk. These
technical measures supplement—not replace—consent, licenses, source records,
and human review.

For AI-assisted media, retain a private provenance record of the approved
brief, source inputs and their rights status, provider, generation or edit
history, reviewer, and approved public use. Do not use third-party reference
material as a public asset or training input unless the required right is
documented.

## Article VIII — Public claim gate

No origin, attribution, ownership, research, competitor, capability, or
historical claim may be published unless a human reviewer confirms:

- the claim is necessary and phrased within its evidence;
- its origin class and confidence level are recorded;
- consent, rights, and privacy limitations permit the planned use;
- no contested or unverified material is being represented as fact;
- required legal, professional, accessibility, and safety review is complete.

If evidence is incomplete, the permitted outcomes are to label it
unverified, defer publication, publish an approved uncertainty statement, or
remove the claim. Silence or uncertainty is preferable to a false origin
claim.

## Article IX — Challenges, disputes, and remediation

Rights, attribution, impersonation, false-origin, or provenance concerns must
be routed to a named human owner. The owner records the report, affected
materials, evidence, interim action, decision rationale, and response date.

For credible issues, AXES may temporarily pause publication, remove an asset
from public display, correct a credit, add a contextual notice, or preserve
the material privately for evidence. Do not publicly accuse a person or
organization of misconduct without adequate evidence and appropriate review.

Contested material is not used for promotion, marketing, rankings,
endorsements, revenue claims, or model training while review is open.

Creator-origin and lineage records may support accurate credit and provenance,
but they do not establish financial value, investment value, scarcity,
authenticity, popularity, social status, citizenship, or permission to sell,
trade, tokenize, award, or otherwise transact in a work.

The Harmonics name does not change this rule. Creator-origin records cannot
assign Harmonics, determine a Harmonics balance, calculate a personal level, or
automatically authorize a transaction, reward, access right, or financial
claim.

A future NUR record may preserve provenance for a UR (Unit of Eternal Origin)
or individual gem only under the same evidence, consent, correction, privacy,
and human-review requirements. The record does not itself establish ownership,
authenticity, financial value, transfer rights, or a right to issue, sell,
award, or trade the item.

## Article X — Registry safeguards

The creator-origin registry is a private, minimum-data record. It must not
become a public identity registry, surveillance system, people-search service,
data broker, reputation score, or automated rights adjudicator. It must not
collect identity documents, biometric data, private addresses, private
contacts, payment data, or unrelated personal details for early pilots.

Access is limited to named operators with a work need. Maintain an access
record, retention period, export/correction/removal route, backup plan, and
incident owner before storing records. Public pages may display only
specifically approved credits and notices.

The KEYSTONE application registry applies these principles to application
source and operational authority. Authority must be explicit, bounded,
human-approved, and revocable; an internal registration cannot create legal or
global authority. See `KEYSTONE_APPLICATION_ORIGIN_REGISTRY.md`.

## Article XI — Oversight and amendments

The founder is the initial constitutional steward. Each active service must
name an accountable human owner for its records and public claims. Review this
constitution at least quarterly and before launching any creator registry,
public archive, AI-media workflow, research program, or service that accepts
submissions.

Amendments require a dated record describing the change, reason, scope,
approver, and whether existing origin records require review. No amendment may
retroactively erase a creator's documented consent, restriction, correction,
or dispute record.
