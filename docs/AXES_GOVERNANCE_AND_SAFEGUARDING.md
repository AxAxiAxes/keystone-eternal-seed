# AXES governance and safeguarding foundation

**Status:** Internal operating foundation; review before each public pilot  
**Recorded:** 2026-09-10  
**Scope:** AXES Contracting, Chichetki, AXOUX, AUXAOUS, AXEOUS, School of Love
& Ethics, AXES Creative Studio, and future public or invite-only services.

## Purpose

This foundation sets the minimum operating rules for any AXES service that
collects information, receives submissions, displays creator work, enables
participation, or introduces a new interactive experience. It is a product
and operations standard, not a substitute for applicable legal, privacy,
employment, youth-safeguarding, tax, or professional advice.

## Operating principles

1. **Human accountability:** A named human owner is responsible for each
   service, moderation decision, data category, vendor relationship, and
   incident response.
2. **Minimum necessary data:** Collect only what is necessary to provide the
   stated service. Do not request sensitive information for exploratory
   experiences.
3. **Clear choice:** Explain what is collected, why, who can see it, and how
   it can be changed, exported, or deleted before collecting it.
4. **No autonomous consequential action:** Agents may assist with drafting,
   classification, and operational signals, but cannot make binding
   moderation, financial, employment, eligibility, safety, or publishing
   decisions.
5. **Truthful scope:** Labels must distinguish public information, invite-only
   pilots, internal prototypes, and future concepts.
6. **Accessible participation:** Public and pilot experiences must support
   keyboard use, readable contrast, text alternatives, captions or
   transcripts for media, and reduced-motion preferences.

## Service readiness record

Before launching or materially changing a service, record:

| Required item | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Owner and purpose | Named accountable owner, audience, and written purpose |
| Access level | Public, authenticated, invite-only, or operator-only |
| Data map | Fields collected, purpose, storage location, viewers, retention, export, and deletion path |
| Content rules | Prohibited conduct, intellectual-property expectations, and review process |
| Safety operations | Named reviewer/moderator, report intake route, escalation owner, and response target |
| Accessibility | Review of navigation, contrast, text alternatives, media captions/transcripts, and motion |
| Vendor record | Provider, approved use, pricing approval, privacy/rights review, and offboarding plan |
| Rollback | How to pause access, remove a feature, export data, and preserve necessary records |

## Data classification

| Class | Examples | Handling rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Approved product descriptions, published articles, approved media | Publish only after source, rights, accessibility, and owner review |
| Internal | Roadmaps, non-sensitive service status, draft copy | Limit to approved operators; do not publish by default |
| Private | Source archives, creator drafts, support records, account configuration, unpublished media | Store in access-controlled systems; do not put in public repositories or prompts |
| Sensitive/restricted | Credentials, payment details, legal records, identity documents, health, financial, intimate, or child-related information | Do not collect for early pilots; use dedicated approved systems and professional review if ever required |

## Content, attribution, and media

- Record the creator/source, rights status, intended use, approval date, and
  accessibility treatment for every public asset.
- Treat user submissions as private until the contributor gives documented
  permission for the exact publication context.
- Never claim ownership of contributor work merely because it was submitted.
- Do not use third-party reference images, music, video, prompts, or designs
  as public assets without a documented right to do so.
- AI-assisted media requires a human review before publication and a private
  record of the brief, provider, generation/edit history, and approved use.

## Moderation and incident response

Interactive or community features must provide:

1. A visible reporting route and clear conduct expectations.
2. A human reviewer with authority to pause, remove, or restrict content.
3. A record of report receipt, action, rationale, and appeal where practical.
4. An escalation path for threats, exploitation, harassment, doxxing,
   impersonation, copyright concerns, or unlawful-content concerns.
5. A temporary pause procedure when staffing, safety, or technical controls
   are inadequate.

Early pilots should favor limited membership, no unrestricted direct messages,
no public location sharing, and no anonymous upload capability.

## Youth and age safeguards

The first learning and creator pilots should be adult-only unless a separate
age-appropriate program is approved. Before admitting minors, AXES needs:

- An age-policy and jurisdiction review.
- Appropriate guardian consent and notice workflows where required.
- Separation of adult and minor interaction spaces.
- Trained human moderators and a documented escalation process.
- No direct adult-minor messaging, location sharing, financial activity, or
  sensitive-profile collection.

## Commerce, professional services, and financial boundaries

- Materials discovery starts as education, supplier references, and human
  inquiries; it is not a checkout or fulfillment system.
- The future AXES Directory is business-only and opt-in. It may publish only
  owner- or authorized-representative-confirmed business name, category,
  service area, website, and public business contact method after human
  review. Its field, lifecycle, correction/removal, renewal, and
  track-specific limits are defined in `AXES_DIRECTORY_DATA_MODEL.md`; it
  must not represent a listing as verified, endorsed, licensed, insured, or
  quality-assured.
- Architectural content remains general education until a responsible licensed
  professional, jurisdiction, and service agreement are defined.
- Chichetki product language may describe design, art, sound, craft, and
  experience but must not make unsupported health, therapeutic, scientific,
  cultural, or intellectual-property claims.
- URNUR remains outside financial, market, crypto, token, custody, payment,
  exchange, or trading activity until written legal approval defines a
  permitted scope.

## Review cadence

Review active pilots monthly and before each major release. Confirm:

- The purpose and audience remain accurate.
- Data collection is still necessary and the delete/export path works.
- Incidents, reports, access requests, and unresolved support items are
  reviewed by a human owner.
- Vendor access, permissions, operating costs, asset rights, and public
  claims remain current.
- The feature should continue, be improved, paused, or retired.

## Pilot launch checklist

A feature can move from planned to pilot only when every applicable item is
complete:

- [ ] Product purpose, audience, and owner documented.
- [ ] Data and access map reviewed.
- [ ] Terms, notices, and consent language prepared as needed.
- [ ] Human support and moderation owner assigned.
- [ ] Asset, rights, and accessibility review complete.
- [ ] Reporting and pause paths tested.
- [ ] Budget and vendor usage approved by a human.
- [ ] Legal/professional requirements reviewed for the actual jurisdiction and
      service scope.
